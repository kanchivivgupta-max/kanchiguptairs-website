About Sri Vishnu Sahasranama

Sri Vishnu Sahasranama (श्री विष्णु सहस्रनाम) is one of the most sacred and widely recited stotras in Hinduism. Found in the Mahabharata (Anushasana Parva, Section 149), it contains 1000 names (Sahasranama = सहस्रनाम, "thousand names") of Lord Vishnu, composed across 108 shlokas.

The stotram is presented as a dialogue between Bhishma Pitamaha and King Yudhishthira on his bed of arrows, as the great war of the Mahabharata draws to a close. When Yudhishthira asks Bhishma for the single best path to prosperity, peace, and liberation, Bhishma replies with the Vishnu Sahasranama — declaring that chanting Vishnu's names is the highest spiritual practice.

Each of the 1000 names is a meditation on a distinct quality, form, or cosmic function of Vishnu — from Vishvam (the universe itself is his form) to Sharanam (the ultimate refuge of all beings). Chanting these names with understanding is considered a complete spiritual practice, conferring clarity, protection, inner peace, and a deep sense of connection to the Divine.

The number 108 carries deep significance across Indian spiritual traditions — from 108 principal Upanishads to 108 beads in a japa mala (prayer garland), 108 pithas (sacred sites), and the 108 dance karanas in classical Indian dance. The 108-verse structure of the Sahasranama is a deliberate and meaningful container for this sacred knowledge.
